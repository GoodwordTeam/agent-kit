# Ideation frameworks

Pick the frameworks that fit the subject. Running every one mechanically produces volume, not
ideas. A framework opens thinking; it never supplies a basis. Every candidate it produces still
carries a `direct:`, `external:` or `reasoned:` basis, and faces the same critique as any other.

The lenses in the skill body (pain and friction, inversion, assumption-breaking, leverage,
cross-domain analogy, constraint-flipping) are the default spread. The frameworks below are ways
to work a lens harder when it comes up thin.

## SCAMPER

Transform something that already exists by applying seven operations:

- **Substitute:** swap a component, process, audience or delivery model.
- **Combine:** merge it with an adjacent capability, or join two things that rarely go together.
- **Adapt:** borrow what another domain or period already does.
- **Modify:** make it ten times larger or smaller; exaggerate one feature; strip it to the minimum.
- **Put to other uses:** who else could use it, and which other problem could it solve?
- **Eliminate:** remove a step or a feature entirely. What is the zero-configuration version?
- **Reverse or rearrange:** run the steps in the opposite order, or move the work between the
  person and the system.

Best for improving an existing product, flow or feature. Weak on greenfield subjects.

## How might we

Reframe a pain point as an opportunity: "How might we [outcome] for [specific person] without
[constraint]?" Write several framings of the same problem; each unlocks different candidates.

- Narrow enough to act on: "help a new user reach their first result in five minutes".
- Broad enough to leave room: not "add a recommendation sidebar".
- Carries a tension that forces invention.

Avoid framings that are too broad ("make users happy"), too narrow ("add a button to settings")
or that embed a solution ("build a support chatbot"). Best for pulling a request anchored on one
solution back to its problem.

## First principles

1. What is known to be true here, not assumed and not conventional?
2. What is being assumed? List every assumption, including the obvious ones.
3. Which assumptions are constraints of the world, and which are habits?
4. From the truths alone, what would be built?

Best when every candidate so far is a small step on the status quo. The truths it rests on are the
candidate's basis, and they are checked like any other.

## Jobs to be done

Look at what the person is trying to accomplish, not what they ask for:

- Functional job: the task being completed.
- Emotional job: how they want to feel doing it.
- Social job: how they want to be seen.

Form: "When I [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]." The real competitor is
whatever else they could hire for that job, often outside the category. Best when it is unclear
the right problem is being solved.

A candidate whose merit turns on the emotional or social job is a claim about human experience.
Critique can say whether it is grounded; it cannot say whether it lands. Mark it as needing a
human evaluator (the skill body cites the ruling).

## Constraint-flipping

Impose a constraint to force a different answer:

- One day to build it. One feature only. The obvious technology is unavailable.
- It must be free forever. The person has never used the product before.
- It must work for a billion people, or for ten.

Best for cutting through a subject that keeps growing or staying vague.

## Pre-mortem

The idea shipped and failed a year from now. Work backwards:

1. List every plausible reason: technical, adoption, team, timing.
2. For each: preventable, or a signal the idea should change?
3. Which failures are acceptable, and which would end it?

Best during critique, on candidates that feel good and have not been pressed. A failure mode the
candidate cannot answer is a rejection reason, stated as one.

## Analogous inspiration

How has another domain solved a structurally similar problem? Which industry, natural system or
historical precedent already faced it? Look for structural similarity ("a two-sided marketplace
solving trust between strangers"), not surface similarity ("X, but for Y"). A real, relevantly
analogous precedent is an `external:` basis; a loose resemblance is not.

Best for generation, when the candidates all look like the obvious approach.
